Balancing Equations.

In a reaction, atoms swap partners but none appear or vanish. That one rule explains why mass is conserved, why equations must balance, and why one reactant can run out before the other.

Key words

Reactants · productsThe substances you start with · the new substances that form. They go on the left and right of the arrow
Conservation of massAtoms are rearranged, never created or destroyed, so the total mass stays the same. A mass change on an open balance means a gas left or joined
SubscriptThe small number after a symbol or bracket. It counts atoms inside the formula. Changing it changes the substance
CoefficientThe big number in front of a formula. It multiplies the whole formula, and it's the only number you change to balance an equation
Balanced equationThe same number of each type of atom on both sides, using the smallest whole-number coefficients
Limiting reactantThe reactant that runs out first. It decides how much product can form; the other reactant is left over
